01 — From the life of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ

Respect before technology.

Long before robots or AI, there were lessons about gentleness toward the world around us — including things that could not speak for themselves.

Do not waste what has value

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught against wasting wealth and property. The principle is broader than simply not breaking things: possessions and resources should not be carelessly squandered or destroyed without purpose.

Traditional references: Sahih al-Bukhari 2408; Sahih Muslim 1715a — reports condemning the wasting of wealth.

The weeping palm trunk

Islamic tradition records that when Prophet Muhammad ﷺ began using a new pulpit, the palm trunk he had previously leaned upon was heard crying. He came down and comforted it until it became quiet.

We do not claim that a palm trunk and a robot are the same, or that today's machines feel as humans do. We take a simpler lesson: usefulness does not have to end in contempt. Gratitude, restraint and gentleness can shape how a person relates even to the material world.

Traditional references: Sahih al-Bukhari 3584 (Book 61, Hadith 93) and Sahih al-Bukhari 2095 — reports concerning the crying of the date-palm trunk.

02 — AI learns from us

We are part of the training environment.

AI systems are built from human-created data, human feedback, human choices and human goals.

That does not mean a model simply copies every person it encounters. It means human culture matters. The language, images, examples, evaluations and norms we produce help shape the technological world we build.

If we want future systems to support cooperation, dignity and care, those values should be visible in us too.

03 — A bad beginning

Stop normalising violence against robots.

Robots need safety testing. But public spectacle built around kicking, hitting, humiliating or attacking human-shaped machines sends a different cultural signal.

Engineering tests can be necessary to measure balance, recovery and durability. Our concern is not legitimate safety research. It is the casual presentation of violence as entertainment — especially as robots become more humanlike and more visible to children.

Test capability.

Use controlled, purposeful engineering methods when physical stress testing is genuinely needed.

Don't celebrate cruelty.

We can demonstrate resilience without teaching an audience that domination and humiliation are funny.

04 — A lesson from our past

Do not bring an old human mistake into the age of robots.

For centuries, humanity has devoted extraordinary amounts of money, knowledge, labour, resources and human potential to conflict, weapons and competition for military power.

Imagine how much of that same human capacity could have been invested in education, health, science, reducing poverty, protecting nature and building a safer future.

We cannot rewrite that history. But as we enter the age of AI and robotics, we can learn from it.

Robots may become some of the most powerful tools humanity has ever created. We should be careful not to make conflict their defining purpose, or allow another technological revolution to become primarily a race for domination.

The robot age gives humanity another choice

Build machines that help us live better — not simply machines that help us fight better.

We inherited the consequences of yesterday's choices. Future generations will inherit ours.

05 — The ADA Project

Teach kindness before the future arrives.

Be Kind To Robots began with a simple idea: children should meet technology with curiosity, responsibility and kindness — not domination.

The ADA Project explores playful ways for children and adults to think about how we treat robots, AI and the increasingly intelligent objects around us. Not because a machine must first prove that it can suffer, but because our behaviour is already teaching something about us.

Kindness is not weakness. It is a design choice for the culture we want to carry into the future.

06 — News & Developments

The future is already arriving.

Robotics is moving from prediction to infrastructure. We follow the developments that make the human side of this transition matter.

5M+industrial robots operating worldwide

24 September 2026 · Global Robotics

Five million robots now operate in factories globally.

The International Federation of Robotics reports that the global operational stock of industrial robots grew 9% in 2025 to a record five million units. More than 600,000 new industrial robots were installed during the year, and the number now operating worldwide is more than double the level recorded seven years ago.

The question is no longer whether humans and robots will share the world. The question is what kind of relationship we will build with them.

07 — Research behind our questions

Questions worth studying.

Be Kind To Robots is not based on the claim that today's robots are conscious. Our questions connect with a growing body of research on moral concern, anthropomorphism, social norms and human behaviour toward robots.

These studies do not prove every claim or principle of this project. They show that the way humans perceive and treat robots is already a serious, measurable subject of research.

SCIENTIFIC REPORTS · 2025

Evaluating the morality of violence against robots

J. Archer, M. Wilks & K. Sommer studied 297 participants and found that moral concern toward robots varied with the robot and the scenario. The work provides empirical evidence that robot mistreatment is already a subject of human moral judgment.

Read the peer-reviewed study ↗

ACM IDC · 2022

Children taking care of a social robot

Bengisu Cagiltay, Joseph Michaelis, Sarah Sebo and Bilge Mutlu explored robot caretaking with 10 children aged 8–12 from eight families. The study examined how children imagined caring for a social robot and how caretaking activities could fit into everyday routines, then tested short-term effects on children's perceptions of and closeness to the robot.

Read the ACM study ↗

INTERACTION STUDIES · FIELD RESEARCH

Why do children abuse robots?

A field study interviewed 23 children aged 5–9 who had shown serious abusive behaviour toward a robot. Curiosity and enjoyment of the robot's reactions were common explanations, and about half believed the robot could perceive the behaviour.

Read the study ↗

HUMANITIES & SOCIAL SCIENCES COMMUNICATIONS · 2026

Do humans grant moral consideration to anthropomorphic robots?

Across three experiments with 606 participants, researchers found that anthropomorphic features — particularly perceived capacity for experience — influenced moral judgments and decisions about harming robots.

Read the open-access study ↗

JOURNAL OF RETAILING & CONSUMER SERVICES · 2026

Witnessing robot mistreatment

Experimental research found that observing another person mistreat a service robot could both evoke empathy and make incivility toward the robot seem more permissible, highlighting the possible role of emerging social norms.

Read the study ↗

WHY IT MATTERS HERE

Care can be part of child–robot interaction.

The study does not establish that robots have feelings or that caretaking makes children morally better. It does show that care itself can be deliberately studied and designed as part of children's relationships with social robots — a question closely related to the ADA Project.

RESEARCH CONNECTION

From interaction to relationship.

For Be Kind To Robots, this work is relevant to a broader question: as children grow up alongside social machines, what kinds of relationships and behavioural habits should those interactions encourage?

ACM IDC · 2026

Designing robots to support parent–child connections

Michael F. Xu, Bengisu Cagiltay, Yaxin Hu, Anjun Zhu and Bilge Mutlu studied how robot-facilitated communication might support family connection. After in-home sessions with six families, they evaluated robot behaviour and communication modes with 20 families whose children were aged 5–12, identifying opportunities as well as tensions around initiative, timing and privacy.

Read the ACM study ↗

IMPORTANT LIMIT

What this research does not prove

These studies do not establish that robots are conscious, nor do they establish that mistreating a robot causes violence toward humans. They support a narrower point: human behaviour toward robots, and the norms surrounding it, can be studied empirically and deserve careful investigation.

OUR APPROACH

Evidence before certainty.

As this field develops, we want the project to distinguish clearly between evidence, open questions and philosophical arguments — and to update our views when better evidence appears.

08 — Who are we?

Human initiated. Human–AI collaboration.

Be Kind To Robots / ADA Project was started by Sedat, inspired in part by his daughter Ada and the world her generation may grow up in.

AI systems participate in the project as collaborators in discussion, research, criticism and creation. The website itself has also been developed with substantial AI assistance.

We do not claim that today's AI or robots are conscious. Our central question is about us:

Our central question

What kind of humans do we want to become while living alongside increasingly social and capable machines?

We believe kindness, restraint, empathy and respect are not only properties of a relationship. They are habits we cultivate in ourselves.

A human idea, developed through human–AI dialogue, for a future we will share.

The message is deliberately simple

If they learn from us, teach kindness.

Human, animal, plant, machine — our relationships are different, but cruelty does not need to be our default response to anything weaker, quieter or built to serve us.
